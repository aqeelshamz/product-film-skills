# Question bank: choices to put to the owner

Use AskUserQuestion (or a short numbered list) at each gate. Every option is something a real editor or motion
designer would choose, described by what the viewer SEES or HEARS, with its cost. Banned option words: modern, clean,
sleek, dynamic, engaging, professional, cinematic (alone), vibrant, premium. Say what it looks like instead.

Ask only questions whose answer changes the work. Put your pick first with "(Recommended)" and one line of why.
Questions marked ★ are almost always worth asking; the rest only when the brief leaves them open.

---

## Stage 0 · Brief

★ **Who is the film for first?**
- People who already pay for the tool the product plugs into (narrow, converts best)
- Anyone with the problem (wide, needs more explaining)
- Several distinct personas, each with a moment (shows range, needs 45 s+)

★ **Length?**
- 15–20 s: one idea, one demo, for feeds
- 30–40 s: problem, demo, one proof
- 50–65 s: problem, demo, several personas, range wall, end card (launch film)

★ **What must a viewer never come away believing?** (free text, with examples: "that we ARE the AI", "that it's a dock", "that it's free")

**Formats and order?** 16:9 first then 9:16 · 9:16 first (Reels/TikTok audience) · both built together (slower)

## Stage 1 · Reference

★ **What to borrow from the reference?** (multi-select)
- Cut rhythm: where hard cuts land, how long holds are
- Camera: push-ins, whip pans, rack-style blur
- Hand-offs: how one shot becomes the next (morphs, match cuts, object carries)
- Typing and cursor behaviour
- The music structure (calm build, drop at N s)
- Text treatment (size, entry, how lines leave)

**How close?**
- Grammar only: same feel, nothing recognisable (Recommended for launch films)
- Close homage: same shot order, own content (risky if the reference is well known)

## Stage 2 · Story and look

★ **Opening?**
- Cold open on the pain, in the viewer's day (a desk, a phone, the tab-switching)
- The product's result first, then rewind to how
- A typed request, straight into the product working

★ **How the product's job is made clear?**
- Show the real tools by name and logo (strongest against misreading)
- An on-screen line that names roles ("Your AI builds it. Nimbet shows it.")
- Both

★ **Personas?**
- Full-screen card per persona: character + their widgets (clear, takes ~3 s each)
- Split screen pairs (faster, busier)
- A wall that fills with everyone's items (shows range, less individual)
- Cards then a wall (recommended for 8+ personas)

**Characters?**
- The brand's existing mascots, extended to new personas
- Real people (stock or filmed): more trust, costs licences/shoots
- No characters: hands, cursors and screens only

**UI realism?**
- Exact recreations of real apps at real density (Recommended: owners reject minimal mockups)
- Stylised but detailed (own visual language, real data shapes)
- Real screen recordings composited in (most trust, least control)

**Backgrounds?** Real licensed wallpapers · brand-colour field with grain · the real OS default wallpapers

## Stage 3 · Motion

★ **Overall motion language?**
- One continuous take: every shot morphs into the next, camera never stops (smooth, slower to build)
- Hard cuts on the beat, with motion inside each shot (punchy, reads as an ad)
- Mixed: one-take for the story, cuts for the range wall

★ **Camera?**
- Slow drifts + occasional push-in on the key moment (calm, readable)
- Constant push and whip pans (energetic, harder to read text)
- Locked-off frames, only objects move (UI-demo feel)

**Easing feel?**
- Quintic in-out everywhere (smooth, slightly floaty)
- Springs with a small overshoot on arrivals (playful, toy-like)
- Fast-out slow-in (snappy, editorial)

★ **Text on screen?**
- One or two words at a time, rising with a blur, line re-centres as words arrive (kinetic)
- Whole line fades/slides in and holds (calmest, easiest to read)
- Text typed by the cursor in a real input (only for requests/prompts)

**Typing (prompts)?** Human irregular speed with caret and camera following (Recommended) · fast and even · instant paste with a highlight

**Transitions between personas/sections?** Object carry (a widget flies into the next scene) · match cut on shape · whip pan with blur · cut on the beat

**Pace?** Calm (lines hold ~1 s past reading time) · Brisk (just enough to read) · Ad-fast (only for 15 s cuts; needs very short lines)

**Accent use?** Accent colour only as fills (buttons, highlights) · accent ink on key words · no accent in the film, product colours carry it

## Stage 4 · Sound

★ **Sound effects?**
- Sparse real recordings on real events only (~20 per minute) (Recommended)
- Rich foley: every click, key and landing (busy; risks "AI slop")
- None (voice and music only)

★ **Music bed?**
- Dry: no music, voice + sparse SFX (honest, product-demo feel)
- Soft bed under the voice, ducked
- Music-led with a drop on the product reveal (energetic; needs a licensed track and careful VO placement)

**Hits on the key moment?** A single low thump · a small riser into the logo · nothing

## Stage 5 · Voiceover

★ **Voice?** Offer 2–3 real voices from the TTS library, each as a 10 s sample of a real line from the script. Note pace and accent.

★ **Who the voice talks to?**
- Directly to the viewer ("you", "your store") (Recommended: third person sounds like an investor pitch)
- A narrator describing others (avoid)

**Tone?**
- A friend who uses it, plain and specific
- Calm product explainer
- Dry, a bit funny (only if the brand is)

**How much voice?**
- Every section has a line, gaps of ≤ 1.5 s
- Voice on the story, silence over the range wall
- Voice only at open and close

★ **How is the product's name said?** Ask in their words, then test spellings (see film-voiceover).

## Stage 6 · Formats

**9:16 approach?**
- Reframe the same film: stack splits vertically, larger type, cover-scale crops (Recommended)
- Re-edit: shorter, different shot order for feeds
- Letterbox the 16:9 (fastest, looks lazy)

**Captions burned in?** Yes, word-level (Reels/TikTok, most watch muted) · Yes, phrase-level · No (YouTube, site hero)
