# Writing a voiceover that doesn't sound AI-made

Every rule here comes from a rejected draft. The examples are from a 65 s desktop-widget launch film (product renamed Nimbet here); the
approved draft was the seventh.

## The tests every draft must pass before the owner sees it

1. **Eyes-closed test.** Read only the transcript, top to bottom, with no picture. It must tell a complete, coherent
   story a listener can follow. Every "it", "they" and "that" points to something just heard.
2. **Frame test.** Each line sits on the frames it plays over (storyboard with REAL frames). When on-screen words
   appear, the voice either says those exact words at that moment or stays quiet.
3. **Stranger test.** Someone who's never heard of the product: what do they think it is after each line? Could any
   pair of adjacent lines mislead them?
4. **Slop scan.** Search the draft for every pattern in the table below. Run the humanizer skill on it.
5. **Claim check.** Every claim has a source in the product (file, doc, test). Write it next to the line.

## Patterns that got drafts rejected

| Rejected | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Your revenue. Your PRs. Your exams." | "your X, your Y" lists read like an ad generator ("your this. your that.") | One concrete example in a full sentence: "Say you run a startup. Ask Claude to show your Stripe revenue." |
| Three parallel sentences per persona | Triads and matching shapes | Vary the shape of each persona line: a statement, a condition, a question. |
| "Here, a founder asks Claude for her revenue." | Describes the scene the viewer can already see | Say what the picture can't: "Your Stripe key goes on the widget, never into the chat." |
| "A developer asks…", "Someone with an online shop…", "A student wants…" | Third person sounds "like we are pitching to an investor" | Talk to the viewer: "If you write code, ask for the pull requests waiting on your review." "Run an online shop? Ask for today's sales." "Got exams coming up? Ask for a countdown to each one." |
| "Ask the AI you already pay for. It's called Nimbet." | The name attaches to the AI: listeners think Nimbet is the AI | "Ask the AI you already pay for to make one, and Nimbet puts it on your desktop." |
| "There's even a pet. Ignore it and it gets hungry." | Written about one tiny item on a wall where nobody can tell it's a pet; the shot's point was range | Understand what each shot is FOR. Over a range wall: "Not sure what you'd want? Ask Claude, and it'll suggest widgets for you." |
| Key, key, key in four lines ("That one needs no key at all.") | One word leaned on line after line | Mention a detail once where it matters; check the transcript for repeated words. |
| Lines that only make sense with the picture | Fails eyes-closed ("feels like unrelated things in between") | Link sections with a thread a listener can follow (habit → ask your AI → it appears → examples → suggestions → platforms → where to get it). |
| "not X but Y", "No setup. No code. No waiting." | Contrast reveals and negation lists | State what happens. |
| A clever closing one-liner | Staged closer | End on the action: what it runs on and where to get it. |
| Proposing "no voiceover" after two rejected drafts | Giving up on the owner's requirement | Write a better draft. Ask what they'd say to a friend if stuck. |

## What the approved draft does

> Most of us open the same tabs all day just to check one number. A desktop widget can show it instead. Ask the AI
> you already pay for to make one, and Nimbet puts it on your desktop. Say you run a startup. Ask Claude to show your
> Stripe revenue. Claude builds the widget. Your Stripe key goes on the widget, never into the chat. If you write
> code, ask for the pull requests waiting on your review. The widget refreshes by itself, so you stop checking
> GitHub. Run an online shop? Ask for today's sales. If the widget isn't quite right, tell Claude what to change. Got
> exams coming up? Ask for a countdown to each one. Pretty much anything you can describe works. Not sure what you'd
> want? Ask Claude, and it'll suggest widgets for you. They work on Android too. Custom widgets your AI builds for
> you. Nimbet runs on Windows, Mac and Android. Get it at nimbet dot app.

- Opens on one habit the viewer really has, not a list.
- Each persona line gives the viewer one useful fact (keys stay out of the chat, it refreshes, you can ask for
  changes) instead of describing the shot.
- Speaks to "you" throughout. The four persona openers have four different shapes.
- Says on-screen words exactly when they're on screen ("Custom widgets your AI builds for you.").
- Hedges honestly where needed ("Pretty much anything").
- Every claim has a source listed under the script.

## Silence

Short gaps (0.5–1.5 s) over a busy picture are fine. Ask the owner whether long gaps feel awkward ("do the silence
makes awkwardness?"). For any section left silent, offer a short line and say why silence might be better; let the
owner choose.
